# The International 2018 — Draft Recap

**Status: first draft, unedited.** Written from the sources listed at the bottom — GosuGamers and JoinDota period articles, Liquipedia rosters/placements, and OpenDota match data pulled via this project's own pipeline. Nothing below is from memory or general knowledge; if a fact isn't traceable to one of those, it isn't in here — it's in the "Didn't know whether to include" section instead. Rewrite the voice freely; this is scaffolding, not prose meant to survive contact with your edit.

Venue: Rogers Arena, Vancouver, Canada — the first TI held outside Seattle's Key Arena, which had hosted every prior International. Key Arena was in the middle of a major renovation deal around this time, and while there's no Valve statement on hand directly confirming it as the reason for the move, that renovation is the clear practical reason Seattle wasn't a viable host for 2018.

Prize pool: $25,532,177 — a record at the time, crowdfunded through Battle Pass sales the way every TI since TI4 had been.

## The season that led in

The 2017–18 Dota Pro Circuit ran through five Majors before anyone touched a bracket in Vancouver, and the first real signal of the year came out of nowhere: **DAC 2018** in April, where Mineski beat back three Chinese teams in the lower bracket and then LGD in the grand final to become the first Southeast Asian team ever to win a Dota 2 Major. It was a genuine changing-of-the-guard moment for a region that had spent years being talked about as the "third world" of competitive Dota. PSG.LGD themselves finished as DAC's runners-up — the first line in what became a real run for them that season.

China's own major, **EPICENTER XL**, went the other way in May — PSG.LGD beat a resurgent Team Liquid 3–1 in the final for their first title as a partnership, with Liquid's semifinal run alone enough to lock their TI8 invite early. A week later, **GESC: Thailand** (a Minor) went to VGJ.Storm, who ran the group stage undefeated.

Then **MDL Changsha Major** in the third week of May turned into a full PSG.LGD coronation — undefeated through the group stage, a clean sweep of VGJ.Storm's Cinderella run to the finals, and with it a direct TI8 invite and back-to-back Major titles inside three weeks. Days later, **ESL One Birmingham** closed out May with Virtus.pro beating OpTic Gaming in the final — VP's third Major title of the era, prompting their own GosuGamers headline to call them a three-time champion outright.

Ahead of that Birmingham Major, on **May 23**, Fnatic made a roster move worth its own line: Saahil "Universe" Arora joined the team, with Kurtis "Aui_2000" Ling coming in as coach for the event. Per your own recollection, this is the moment tied to the "Pizza Party" meme — EternalEnvy, Fnatic's captain at the time, reportedly kicking players while the team had a pizza party to make room for the move. Worth writing that part in your own words with the actual detail, since it's not something the fetched sources confirm directly.

June belonged to the **China Supermajor**, a farewell tour of sorts for old faces (Na'Vi's LeBronDota reportedly benched for the event) and a coming-out party for Team Liquid, who beat back OpTic and PSG.LGD's own bracket run to take the title — their second Major-level trophy of the season heading into the TI8 qualifiers.

### OG's collapse, and the rebuild that won it all

The single most important story of the pre-TI season didn't happen on a Major stage. On **May 27**, Evil Geniuses cut Misery and Fear loose after a rough Birmingham exit. The next day, **May 28**, OG announced that Fly and s4 — the captain and a founding member of the roster — were leaving to join EG outright, in what GosuGamers described as "one brotherhood... reunited, another... broken," a roster swap between two organizations rather than a normal departure.

That left OG needing to rebuild from close to scratch with the TI8 qualifiers bearing down. On **June 4**, they announced their answer: Anathan "ana" Pham was brought back to the carry position, and an unproven Finnish player, Topias "Topson" Taavitsainen, was recruited for mid. Three weeks later, on **June 25**, GosuGamers ran the headline "OG reborn: new roster qualifies for TI8" — the rebuilt roster had gone through Europe's regional qualifier without dropping a game that mattered enough to make news otherwise.

Nobody at the time was writing this up as a title favorite. It's the version of OG that would go on to win the whole thing ten weeks later.

### The qualifiers, and the money climbing

Through June, the open and regional qualifiers filled out the field: Team Serenity became the first team to officially qualify (June 20), followed in short order by Winstrike, Invictus Gaming, VGJ.Storm, Fnatic, paiN Gaming, TNC Predator, Evil Geniuses, and OpTic Gaming — with Thunder Predator disqualified from the South America qualifier (June 22) over macro use on Huskar and Meepo.

The Battle Pass crowdfunding numbers were treated as their own running story all season: past the $20 million mark with six weeks still to go (July 3), and by **August 20** — the tournament already underway — the pool had officially passed the previous year's $24,787,916 to become, in GosuGamers' words, "the world's largest electronic sports championship." It closed at $25,532,177.

## The event

### Group stage (Aug 15–19)

Sixteen teams (later effectively eighteen with the "17th–18th" tie), split into two groups of eight, with the top two from each securing a direct seed into the upper bracket and the bottom two eliminated outright — a harsher group format than some previous years, per the period coverage.

Evil Geniuses opened Day 1 on top of Group A while OG and VGJ.Thunder stumbled out of the gate. VGJ.Storm dominated Group B on both Day 2 and Day 3 of the coverage on hand, finishing as the group's top seed — which made it notable when, on **August 19**, in the group stage's final hours, Valve penalized them heading into the Main Event. The rules allowed five players and one coach in the draft room; VGJ.Storm had their main coach, Aaron "Clairvoyance" Kim, in the booth alongside co-coach/analyst Ilya "Illidan" Pivcaev — after, by their account, asking a PGL staffer who told them he didn't mind as long as both left by the end of the draft. Valve found no malicious intent but penalized them anyway: no coach allowed for their first playoff match (against OG), plus a Level 2 drafting penalty cutting 70 seconds from their reserve time.

Group stage elimination day (Aug 19) took out Invictus Gaming (after a tiebreaker series against Winstrike) and paiN Gaming — the first Brazilian team to ever attend a TI, whose run ended in the group stage.

### Main Event, day by day

The single/double-elimination bracket ran August 20–26. In rough order:

- **Aug 21** — First-round exits: Fnatic, TNC Predator, Newbee, and VGJ.Thunder all eliminated.
- **Aug 22** — Team Serenity and Mineski eliminated. OG and EG both secure top-6 finishes.
- **Aug 23** — WinStrike and Vici Gaming eliminated. PSG.LGD and OG both win through in the upper bracket. The same day, GosuGamers ran a retrospective on Newbee — TI7's runners-up the year before, eliminated two days earlier on Aug 21 — asking what had gone wrong for them across the season.
- **Aug 24** — Team Liquid sweep Team Secret 2–0, ending Secret's run. Virtus.pro, OpTic Gaming, and VGJ.Storm are all eliminated the same day — North America's two remaining hopes and one of the tournament's pre-event favorites, gone within hours of each other. The OG–PSG.LGD upper bracket final also lands here: a three-game series, OG taking it 2–1 (games at 48.9, 37.0, and 58.1 minutes) to send the last remaining Chinese team at the event down to the lower bracket.
- **Aug 25** — The heaviest day of the tournament. Team Liquid fall to Evil Geniuses in the lower bracket semifinal (0–2), ending Liquid's run in 4th. PSG.LGD beat EG 2–0 in a series GosuGamers called "quick" and full of "fantastic drafts" to reach the Grand Finals for the second time — eliminating EG in 3rd. That set the stage: OG vs. PSG.LGD for the Aegis, a rematch of the upper-bracket final three days earlier. The same day, Valve announced **TI9 would be held in Shanghai** — the first time the event would leave North America/Europe — and shipped two new heroes, Grimstroke (live immediately) and Mars (announced for later).

### The Grand Finals — OG vs. PSG.LGD, best of 5

Per this project's own OpenDota-derived match data:

| Game | Score (radiant–dire) | Winner | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | OG 34 – 27 LGD | **OG** | 34.6 min |
| 2 | LGD 45 – 19 OG | **LGD** | 38.1 min |
| 3 | OG 25 – 50 LGD | **LGD** | 34.4 min |
| 4 | LGD 58 – 49 OG | **OG** | 65.3 min |
| 5 | LGD 34 – 35 OG | **OG** | 36.5 min |

OG took it 3–2. Game 4 alone ran past the hour mark with LGD actually out-killing OG 58–49 and still losing — the kind of scoreline that usually means a networth/objectives win through the back door rather than a clean fight. JoinDota's own MVP-vote writeup for the tournament singles out "ana's turnaround Ember Spirit play to save the lives of OG in Game 5" as the community's leading candidate for the defining moment of the series — a genuine, sourced detail, though the pipeline doesn't currently have full hero-draft data per game to confirm what else was on OG's side of that draft. A separate JoinDota retrospective calls OG's Game 5 draft a "surprise" that "nobody would have expected them to win with."

OG's own team account tweeted, in the moment: "Against all odds, we stood united, fought until the very end and became your #TI8 Champions! The Dream is now real."

The prize split: OG **1st**, $11,234,158 (44%). PSG.LGD **2nd**, $4,085,148 (16%). Evil Geniuses **3rd**, $2,680,879. Team Liquid **4th**, $1,787,252. Virtus.pro and Team Secret shared **5th–6th**. OpTic Gaming and VGJ.Storm shared **7th–8th**. Team Serenity, Mineski, Winstrike, and Vici Gaming shared **9th–12th**. Fnatic, TNC Predator, Newbee, and VGJ.Thunder shared **13th–16th**. Invictus Gaming and paiN Gaming shared **17th–18th**.

The community's own MVP vote (JoinDota) landed on **ana** — his Grand Finals performance specifically cited as the deciding factor, an especially notable outcome given ana had been out of competitive Dota entirely before OG brought him back for the qualifiers ten weeks earlier.

### Why this mattered beyond the trophy

A regional-newspaper roundup GosuGamers/JoinDota ran the week after cited Finnish press covering Topson and JerAx (both Finnish nationals) making the country's mainstream news off the back of the win — a detail worth keeping for texture on how far outside the usual esports press this result traveled.

Viewership numbers for TI8 were reported (JoinDota, citing Esports Charts data) as up significantly over TI7's, alongside the record prize pool — the two records the period coverage kept pairing together as evidence of the game's growth that year.

## Immediately after

The post-TI shuffle started before the confetti settled:

- **Aug 28** — compLexity begins shedding its roster (Adam out first).
- **Aug 29** — Universe and Aui_2000 — the pairing that had been split up when Universe went to Fnatic in SEA and Aui went home to coach — reunite again, this time forming a new North American stack together with Resolut1on.
- **Aug 30–31** — compLexity fully implodes (Moo and Moonmeander both out inside two days).
- **Sept 1** — Two of the scene's longest-tenured stars step away from their teams: EternalEnvy leaves Fnatic and the SEA region entirely, and — in the piece with arguably the most emotional weight in the whole dataset — **Dendi will not be part of Na'Vi's roster for the first time in eight years.** Na'Vi's own statement: "Thank you for the 8 years of support and trust."

Virtus.pro's coach, Artstyle, and OG's coach, ppasarel, both stayed in place — the rest of the top 4 all changed something within two weeks of the Aegis being handed out. Notably, Virtus.pro had already locked their entire roster to contracts through the end of 2019 back on **Aug 10, ahead of the event** — the one team in the top tier that walked into TI8 having already settled its future either way.

## The numbers, for a stats sidebar

- **Patch 7.19** — the client version the tournament was played on. (Not from the GosuGamers/JoinDota/Liquipedia data on hand — confirmed via a live web search against NAVI's own publication about the post-TI8 7.19b patch and GosuGamers' own later TI8 retrospective piece.)
- **195 matches** played across group stage + Main Event (190 with full parsed data).
- Average game length: **39.6 minutes** (median 37.7). Longest: 81.9 minutes. Shortest: 17.0 minutes.
- **56.7 kills per game**, on average.
- Roshan killed **2.1 times per game** on average.
- First blood, on average, at **1:43** into the game.
- **110 distinct heroes** picked across the event.
- Most picked: **Vengeful Spirit** (89 games, 53.9% win rate), Tiny (78, 41.0%), Mirana (74, 54.1%), Wraith King (66, 51.5%), Dark Willow (66, 56.1%).
- Most banned, by a wide margin: **Weaver** — banned 117 times against only 62 picks, a hero the field was clearly more scared of than it was willing to play.
- Highest average GPM (min. 5 games): **Miracle-** (625.9), SumaiL (619.4), Ame (605.5).
